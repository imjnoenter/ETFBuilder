# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Retail investors — individuals managing their own money, exploring ETFs, and constructing personal portfolios. They range from curious beginners to self-directed enthusiasts. They're typically on desktop or tablet, often in "research mode" (comparing options, reading metrics) or "build mode" (assembling and adjusting a portfolio). They want to feel capable, not overwhelmed.

## Product Purpose

ETFbuilder combines ETF screening and portfolio construction into a single tool. Users search, filter, and compare ETFs by criteria (expense ratio, risk, yield, asset class), then add selections to a portfolio, adjust weights with sliders, and see allocation, blended metrics, and historical performance update instantly. The full US ETF universe (~5,300 funds) is searchable; a curated core of ~80 funds loads with rich detail, while any ticker can be hydrated on-demand via a Yahoo Finance proxy. Allocation is hard-capped at 100% — sliders stop at the remaining headroom. Light and dark themes with persistence. The goal: make portfolio-building feel hands-on and rewarding rather than clinical.

## Brand Personality

**Smart, playful, empowering.** The interface should feel like a well-designed game — interactive, responsive to every action, with clear visual feedback and a sense of progression. Not gamified in the cheap sense (no badges, no streaks), but genuinely fun to use the way a great puzzle or builder game is. Users should feel sharper after using it.

## Anti-references

- Dense terminal-style interfaces (Bloomberg) — information should be layered and progressive, not all-at-once
- Corporate fintech aesthetic (navy suits, gold accents, stock photography of handshakes) — this is a tool for individuals, not institutions
- Cheap gamification (confetti, achievement popups, social pressure) — the delight comes from the interaction design itself, not reward mechanics bolted on

## Design Principles

1. **Direct manipulation over forms.** Drag, resize, reorder. Every interaction should feel physical and immediate — sliders over input fields, drag-and-drop over dropdowns, inline editing over modals.
2. **Progressive disclosure.** Show the essential, reveal the detailed. A beginner sees a clean builder; a power user finds depth without clutter.
3. **Feedback is instant.** Every action produces a visible, meaningful response. Adjust a weight — the chart reflows. Hover an ETF — see how it changes the portfolio. No dead clicks.
4. **Clarity through visualization.** Numbers tell; charts show. Default to visual representations of data (allocation donuts, performance sparklines, overlap matrices) with numbers as supporting detail.
5. **Earned confidence.** The tool should make users feel competent, not dependent. Explain what metrics mean in context. Show the "why" behind suggestions. Build understanding, not just portfolios.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best practices throughout — good contrast ratios, keyboard navigability, screen reader support, reduced-motion alternatives for animations. No formal WCAG compliance target, but AA contrast minimums treated as a floor.
